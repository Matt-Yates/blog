#1

Cutting Board

Beginner

The foundational woodworking project. Edge-grain construction teaches panel gluing, squaring, and food-safe finishing. Built from Ana White's standard cutting board plan.

Adjust Dimensions

Build Plan — Adjusted Cut List

Cut List
  • [maple] × 3⅝" × [len]" — Maple ([maple] strips)
  • [walnut] × 3⅝" × [len]" — Walnut ([walnut] strips, alternating)
Materials
  • Wood glue (1 bottle)
  • Clamps (4+ bar clamps)
  • Food-safe mineral oil (16 oz)
  • 220-grit sandpaper

Steps

  1. Cut strips to width. Rip maple and walnut boards to 3⅝" wide on the table saw. You need [strips] strips total — [maple] maple, [walnut] walnut.
  2. Arrange in pattern. Lay strips face-up, alternating maple and walnut. Flip every other strip end-for-end to hide end-grain pattern. Add scrap spacer on each end.
  3. Glue the panel. Apply thin glue to each joint, clamp with spreaders. Wipe squeeze-out with damp cloth. Let dry 24 hours.
  4. Cut to final size. Trim to [dims]. Check square with diagonal measurement — both must match.
  5. Sand. Progress through 120 → 180 → 220 grit. Sand with the grain. Board should feel like glass at 220.
  6. Round edges. Use a ⅛" round-over bit on router table or sand by hand.
  7. Apply finish. Coat with food-safe mineral oil. Let soak 30 min, wipe excess. Repeat 3 times over 3 days.
Tools: Table saw, clamps, router (optional), sandpaper, mineral oil

#2

Floating Shelves

Beginner

Minimal materials, maximum visual impact. A hidden-bracket shelf teaches precision cutting and wall-mount hardware. Built from Ana White's floating shelf plans.

Adjust Dimensions

Build Plan — Adjusted Cut List

Cut List
  • 1 × 1×10 × [face]" — Shelf face
  • 2 × 1×4 × [arms]" — L-bracket arms (behind face)
  • 2 × M6×40mm L-brackets
Materials
  • 1¼" wood screws (6)
  • Wall anchors (2, if studs don't align)
  • Spray stain or polyurethane
  • 220-grit sandpaper

Steps

  1. Cut the shelf face. Rip 1×10 to [face"] wide. Fits inside standard L-bracket (8" width) with gap for expansion.
  2. Cut bracket arms. Cut two 1×4 pieces to [arms"] long. These slide behind shelf face to create floating illusion.
  3. Glue and clamp. Center bracket arms on back edge of shelf face. Apply glue, clamp, let dry 2 hours.
  4. Sand and finish. Sand all surfaces to 220 grit. Apply stain or clear coat.
  5. Find wall studs. Use stud finder. Mark center of each stud.
  6. Mount brackets. Hold shelf against wall at desired height. Mark screw holes. Pre-drill, drive screws. Use level — critical.
Tools: Table saw or circular saw, drill, level, clamps, stud finder

#3

Planter Box

Beginner

The forgiving first build. A simple box teaches assembly, angles, and material selection. Built from Ana White's raised planter box plan (cedar fence pickets).

Adjust Dimensions

Build Plan — Adjusted Cut List

Cut List
  • 2 × 1×6 × [frontBack]" — Front & Back panels (exterior)
  • 2 × 1×6 × [sides]" — Side panels (exterior)
  • 1 × plywood [dims] — Bottom panel (½" exterior)
Materials
  • Exterior wood glue
  • 2" exterior screws (12)
  • Landscaping fabric (1 ft²)
  • ¼" drill bit (drainage holes)

Steps

  1. Cut panels. Front/back at [frontBack"], sides at [sides"]. Sides fit inside front and back for [hei"] depth.
  2. Drainage holes. Drill four ¼" holes in bottom panel, spaced evenly. Non-negotiable.
  3. Assemble box. Apply glue to each joint. Drive screws through front panel into sides. Repeat for back. Use clamps to keep square.
  4. Nail on bottom. Slide bottom panel into place under side panels. Screw up through bottom into sides.
  5. Line with fabric. Cut landscaping fabric to fit inside bottom. Staple in place.
  6. Fill and plant. Add soil, plant, enjoy. Cedar or redwood last longest outdoors.
Tools: Miter saw or table saw, drill, 2" screws, clamps, staple gun

#4

Side Table

Intermediate

The classic first furniture piece. Bridges small crafts and real furniture. Built from Ana White's side table plans (2×4 construction).

Adjust Dimensions

Build Plan — Adjusted Cut List

Cut List
  • 4 × 1×4 × [legs]" — Legs (height minus top thickness)
  • 2 × 1×4 × [sideRails]" — Side rails
  • 2 × 1×4 × [shortRails]" — Front/back rails
  • 1 × 1×10 × [dims] — Table top
Materials
  • 1½" pocket hole screws (16)
  • 2" wood screws (4)
  • Wood stain or polyurethane
  • 120 → 220 grit sandpaper

Steps

  1. Drill pocket holes. On each end of the side rails, drill 4 pocket holes.
  2. Build two leg assemblies. Attach each side rail between two legs using pocket holes. Glue + screws. Clamp square. Repeat for other side.
  3. Connect the sides. Attach the two leg assemblies together using remaining 1×4 as front and back rail. Pocket holes through legs.
  4. Flip and attach top. Flip frame upside down. Center table top on it. Screw down through frame into top from underneath.
  5. Sand. Sand top surface from 120 to 220 grit. Sand frame edges to remove saw marks.
  6. Finish. Apply stain if desired. Let dry 4 hrs. Apply polyurethane (2 coats, sanding between).
Tools: Table saw, drill, pocket hole jig, clamps, sander, polyurethane

#5

Sawhorses (Heavy Duty 2×4)

Beginner

Ana White's heavy duty 2×4 sawhorses — built from two 2×4s and a 1×3 furring strip. No special brackets. Used in construction for over 10 years. Zero wobble.

Adjust Dimensions (per sawhorse)

Build Plan — Adjusted Cut List (×2 sawhorses)

Cut List
  • 4 × 2×4 @ [leg1"] — Legs (15° angled cuts)
  • 4 × 2×4 @ [leg2"] — Lower cross supports
  • 8 × 2×4 @ [sides"] — Side supports (15° cuts)
  • 6 × 2×4 @ [tops"] — Top + cross braces
  • 4 × 1×3 @ [beams"] — Top beams (18° angled cuts)
Materials
  • 2½" structural screws (24)
  • 1½" wood screws (8)
  • Wood glue

Steps (Ana White plan)

  1. Attach bottom to center piece. Screw bottom piece to center piece, aligning center of bottom with center piece. Use 2½" screws.
  2. Attach legs to bottom. Screw legs to bottom center piece 8" up from bottom. Screw tops of legs to center piece. Build two leg sets per sawhorse.
  3. Attach top 2×4s and bottom support. Screw top 2×4s and bottom support 2×4 between leg sets. Use 2½" screws.
  4. Add cross pieces. Place cross pieces inside sawhorse, attach with 1½" screws.
  5. Mount top beam. 1×3 on top, angled at 18°. Screw through legs into beam.
Tools: Compound miter saw, drill, clamps, speed square, tape measure

#1

Floor-to-Ceiling Shelving Unit

Intermediate

Ana White's Ultimate Garage Shelving — 20' × 8' × 27" using metal angle supports and 2×4 ladders. Built from Kreg's Garage Storage & Work Center plans (8' × 6' × 24").

Adjust Dimensions

Build Plan — Adjusted Cut List

Cut List
  • 4 × 2×4 × [posts"] — Side posts (full height)
  • [rungs] × 2×4 × [21"] — Shelf supports (vertical rungs between legs)
  • 4 × 2×4 × [rails"] — Horizontal rails (top + bottom)
  • [shelves] × ¾" plywood × [shelfDims] — Shelf surfaces (ripped in half)
  • 1 × ¾" plywood × [shelfDims] — Back panel
Materials
  • 1¼" pocket hole screws (80+)
  • 3" deck screws (16)
  • Wood glue
  • Wall anchors or lag bolts (4)

Steps

  1. Build ladder supports. Two 2×4 side posts, spaced 21" apart. Attach shelf rungs 1½" below desired shelf heights. Ana White uses 5-6' spans between ladders.
  2. Repeat for second ladder. Make identical. Check diagonal measurements match.
  3. Connect with rails. Top and bottom rails go across both ladders. Pocket holes through posts into rails.
  4. Add plywood shelves. Measure inside width, rip plywood to fit. Screw down through angle/shelf boards.
  5. Add back panel. Screw plywood to back of unit. Prevents racking. Screws every 12".
  6. Move into place and anchor. Slide against wall. Anchor into studs with 3" deck screws. Shim if floor is uneven.
Tools: Table saw or circular saw, drill, pocket hole jig, level, 4ft+ clamps, stud finder

Fold-Down Workbench

Beginner

Ana White's folding workbench — takes up 4 sq ft folded, unfolds to full workspace. Features storage compartments that fold with it.

Adjust Dimensions

Build Plan — Adjusted Cut List

Cut List
  • 1 × ¾" plywood × [surface] — Work surface
  • 2 × 2×4 × [legs"] — Side supports (folded position)
  • 2 × 2×4 × [mounts"] — Mounting blocks (wall studs)
  • 4 × 2×4 × [storageSides"] — Storage compartment sides
  • 2 × 2×4 × [storageBottoms"] — Storage compartments bottom
Materials
  • 2× 270° continuous hinges (heavy duty)
  • 3" structural screws (16)
  • Wood glue
  • Support arm with gas strut or chain + hook

Steps

  1. Cut work surface. Rip plywood to [24"] wide. Length [48"]. Sand edges smooth.
  2. Attach hinges to bench. Screw one side of each continuous hinge to edge of work surface. Position 8" from each end.
  3. Build storage compartments. Build box frames from 2×4s. Attach to work surface underside. These store tools when folded.
  4. Find and mark wall studs. Two studs spaced 16" apart. Mounting blocks attach here.
  5. Mount blocks to wall. Screw 2×4 blocks into studs. Position at work level (~36" from floor).
  6. Attach hinges to wall blocks. Screw other side of hinges to wall blocks. Bench should fold flat.
  7. Add support arm. Install gas strut or wooden prop. Holds bench at working angle.
Tools: Circular saw, drill, level, 4ft+ clamps, speed square

#3

Pegboard Tool Wall

Beginner

One weekend. Transforms a blank wall into an organized tool display. Built with 1×4 framing and ¼" pegboard. Family Handyman's most-shared garage project.

Adjust Dimensions

Build Plan — Adjusted Cut List

Cut List
  • 1 × ¼" pegboard × [board] (cut to size)
  • 2 × 1×4 × [topBottom]" — Frame sides (top + bottom)
  • 2 × 1×4 × [sides]" — Frame sides (left + right)
Materials
  • 1½" wood screws (20+)
  • Pegboard hooks (assorted set)
  • Wood glue
  • Wall anchors or lag bolts (4)

Steps

  1. Cut pegboard to size. Measure wall space. Cut with jigsaw or circular saw. Score and snap for cleaner cuts.
  2. Build frame. Assemble rectangular frame from 1×4s. Frame should be slightly larger than pegboard. Glue + screw corners.
  3. Paint or stain frame. Do this before attaching pegboard. Dark stain for workshop vibe, white paint for garage.
  4. Attach pegboard to frame. Lay pegboard face-down. Place frame on top. Screw through frame into pegboard from back. Space screws every 6".
  5. Mount to wall. Hold frame against wall at comfortable height. Find studs. Screw through back of frame into studs.
  6. Arrange tools. Hang wrenches, pliers, screwdrivers. Group by function. Everything reachable without moving anything else.
Tools: Jigsaw or circular saw, drill, level, speed square
#4

Corner Storage Cabinet

Intermediate

Uses dead corner space. Plywood and pocket holes create enclosed storage for cans, bins, bulk items. Built from Woodshop Diaries' corner cabinet plans.

Adjust Dimensions

Build Plan — Adjusted Cut List

Cut List
  • 2 × ¾" plywood × [sides] — Side panels (angled at 45°)
  • 1 × ¾" plywood × [back] — Back panel
  • 1 × ¾" plywood × [top] — Top panel
  • 2 × ¾" plywood × [doors] — Doors (mitered edges)
  • 1 × ¾" plywood × [shelf] — Interior shelf
Materials
  • 1¼" pocket hole screws (40+)
  • 2× cabinet door hinges (pair)
  • 2× magnetic door catches
  • 1¾" wood screws (4, wall anchor)

Steps

  1. Cut side panels to angle. Each side is a right triangle. Cut angled edge at 45° — two sides meet at 90° in corner.
  2. Build box. Attach back panel to sides using pocket holes. Then attach top panel. Open box shape with two angled sides and back.
  3. Add interior shelf. Cut shelf to fit inside box. Pocket hole screws from sides into shelf. Position at waist level.
  4. Build and hang doors. Cut doors to cover front opening. Attach hinges to doors, mount to cabinet frame. Install magnetic catches.
  5. Finish. Sand all surfaces. Semi-gloss enamel for garage — easy to wipe clean.
  6. Anchor to wall. Screw through back panel into wall studs. Corner cabinets tip forward if loaded heavily.
Tools: Table saw, drill, pocket hole jig, clamps, sander, circular saw (angled cuts)

#5

Bike & Long-Tool Wall Rack

Beginner

Under $20 in materials. Wall-mounted rack for bikes, rakes, shovels, hoses. Family Handyman's most-shared garage project. One-hour build.

Adjust Dimensions

Build Plan — Adjusted Cut List

Cut List
  • 1 × 2×4 × [rail"] — Main horizontal rail
  • [blocks] × 2×4 × [8"] — Support blocks (2 per hook position)
  • [hooks] × 2×4 × [4"] — Hook arms (cut at 45° angle)
Materials
  • 2½" wood screws (20+)
  • 3" structural screws (4, into studs)
  • Wood glue
  • 4× heavy-duty wall anchors or lag bolts

Steps

  1. Cut main rail. Cut 2×4 to [72"] — fits wall space. Measure stud center to stud center.
  2. Cut hook arms. Cut four 4" pieces at 45° angle on one end. V-notch that holds bike tires. Angle is critical — without it, bikes slide off.
  3. Assemble hooks to rail. Glue and screw each hook arm to bottom of rail at marked positions. Angled end faces outward.
  4. Add support blocks. Cut 2×4 blocks to fit between rail and wall. Screw through rail into blocks. Prevents sagging.
  5. Mount to wall. Position rail at [60"] height (top of bike tire below shoulder level). Screw into wall studs. Lag bolts if studs are 24" apart.
  6. Load it up. Hang bikes first (heaviest). Then rakes, shovels, hoses. Leave empty hooks for future.
Tools: Circular saw or miter saw, drill, level, speed square
