Planned Projects

Top 5 beginner-to-intermediate woodworking projects, ranked by community recommendation across Reddit, YouTube, instructional sites, and woodworking books

Methodology: Evaluated across 15+ Reddit threads (r/woodworking, r/beginnerwoodworking), major YouTube woodworking channels, Ana White's plans, Family Handyman, and books including Complete Guide to Woodworking (Chris Tribe), The Practical Woodworker (Stephen Corbett), and The Handbuilt Home (Ana White). Ranked by frequency of recommendation, skill value, practical utility, and beginner accessibility.

#1

Cutting Board

Beginner

The most consistently recommended first project across every source. Teaches measuring, cross-cutting, edge-gluing, squaring, and food-safe finishing — the foundational skills everything else builds on.

  • Cross-cutting & rip cuts
  • Edge-gluing panels
  • Squaring a panel
  • Food-safe finishing
Tools: Table saw (or miter saw), clamps, sandpaper
#2

Floating Shelves

Beginner

Ana White's most popular plans. Minimal materials, high visual impact, and teaches precision cutting, sanding, and wall-mount hardware. Multiple sizes = multiple builds with different challenges.

  • Precision cutting
  • Hidden hardware mounting
  • Edge finishing
  • Level installation
Tools: Miter saw or table saw, drill, level, clamps
#3

Planters / Plant Boxes

Beginner

Top 3 most recommended project across Reddit and instructional sites. Teaches basic box construction, assembly at angles, and outdoor material selection. Low stakes — mistakes are forgiving.

  • Box construction
  • 45° or butt joints
  • Outdoor material selection
  • Drainage planning
Tools: Miter saw or table saw, drill, screws, sandpaper
#4

Side Table

Intermediate

The classic first furniture piece. Bridges the gap between small crafts and real furniture. Teaches joinery, leg attachment, and structural integrity — the skills that unlock cabinets and tables.

  • Table leg joinery (pocket holes or mortise & tenon)
  • Appliance top attachment
  • Level & square assembly
  • Finish staining / oiling
Tools: Table saw, drill, pocket hole jig (optional), clamps, sander
#5

Workbench / Sawhorses

The meta-project: build the thing that helps you build everything else. Heavily recommended on Reddit as a true first project that immediately improves every subsequent build. Simple construction, massive practical value.

  • Long cross-cutting
  • Structural assembly
  • Weight & stability design
  • Practical ergonomics
Tools: Table saw or circular saw, drill, 4ft+ clamps, level

Garage Organization

Top 5 woodworking projects for garage storage and organization, ranked by community recommendation across Reddit, Ana White, Kreg, Family Handyman, and YouTube

Methodology: Evaluated across r/woodworking_DIY, r/BeginnerWoodWorking, Ana White's plans, Kreg Tool plans, Family Handyman, and major woodworking YouTube channels. Ranked by practical impact on garage organization, beginner accessibility, material cost efficiency, and community enthusiasm (Reddit upvotes, YouTube comments).

#1

Floor-to-Ceiling Shelving Unit

Intermediate

The highest-impact single project for a garage. Ana White's "Ultimate Garage Shelving" and Kreg's "Garage Storage and Work Center" are the two most popular plans — both use simple plywood and 2x4s, cost under $150, and deliver 30-40 feet of storage in a 6-8 foot footprint. Wall-mounted or floor-standing, it clears the floor for a car and everything else goes up.

  • Long rip cuts (plywood)
  • Pocket-hole joinery
  • Wall stud finding & mounting
  • Load distribution design
Tools: Table saw or circular saw, drill, pocket hole jig, level, 4ft+ clamps
#2

Fold-Down Workbench / Wall Bench

Beginner

The #1 viral garage project on Reddit — multiple threads with dozens of builds and enthusiastic comments. A shelf that folds down into a workbench when you need it, folds flat when you don't. One user built theirs in a weekend and said it "saved my garage and my sanity." Uses simple hinges and 2x4s — no fancy hardware needed.

  • Hinge mounting & alignment
  • Support brace design
  • Surface finishing
  • Space-saving design thinking
Tools: Miter saw or table saw, drill, level, clamps
#3

Pegboard Tool Wall

Beginner

Recommended by virtually every garage makeover video and post. A wooden-framed pegboard panel transforms a blank wall into an organized tool display. Jen Woodhouse, Jonny Builds, and others make this one of the easiest weekend projects with outsized impact on daily usability.

  • Frame construction
  • Pegboard mounting
  • Layout & spacing
  • Paint / stain finish
Tools: Miter saw or table saw, drill, level, screwdriver
#4

Corner Storage Cabinet

Intermediate

Woodshop Diaries' garage makeover series made these corner cabinets extremely popular. Uses dead corner space (usually wasted), plywood sheets, and pocket holes to create enclosed storage for cans, bins, and bulk items. Covers the "clutter that doesn't belong on display" problem.

  • Box construction with doors
  • Corner fit / angled cuts
  • Hinge mounting
  • Interior shelf adjustment
Tools: Table saw, drill, pocket hole jig, clamps, sander
#5

Bike & Long-Tool Wall Rack

Beginner

Simple but transformative. A wall-mounted rack for bikes, rakes, shovels, and long-handled tools clears a huge amount of floor space for under $20 in materials. Family Handyman's list includes multiple versions — from pallet-based to 2x4 slotted designs. One-hour build.

  • Simple joinery
  • Angle cutting for bike forks
  • Wall anchoring
  • Ergonomic height planning
Tools: Miter saw, drill, level, screws
